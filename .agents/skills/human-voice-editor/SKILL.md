# Human Voice Editor

The goal is not to imitate mistakes. The goal is to make the prose feel authored.

## First principle

A human-sounding paragraph usually has a point of view about what matters. It does not merely present information in polished sequence.

Before rewriting, identify what the writer actually wants the reader to notice, which sentence carries the judgment, which details are necessary, and what can be deleted without losing meaning.

## Common machine-like patterns

Repair these when they appear repeatedly: similar sentence lengths and structures; perfectly symmetrical lists and repeated groups of three; paragraphs that all open and close the same way; generic evaluative phrases; excessive signposting such as "Furthermore" or "It is important to note"; over-explaining obvious links; stacks of abstract nouns; prestige vocabulary with no analytical function; fake balance where every claim gets an automatic counterpoint; and conclusions that simply restate the introduction.

## Make prose feel authored

Prefer concrete verbs, selective emphasis, sentence-length variation that follows the thought, direct judgments when the evidence supports them, semantic rather than decorative transitions, paragraph endings that lead into the next idea, specific nouns over vague abstractions, and occasional short sentences when a point genuinely deserves emphasis.

## Preserve the writer

Do not replace the user's voice with a generic "human" voice. If the user provides samples, follow their level of formality, sentence density, preferred vocabulary, and degree of directness.

Do not add fake anecdotes, opinions, uncertainty, slang, typos, or personal experience. Do not deliberately damage grammar to appear human.

## Read-aloud test

After editing, ask whether a competent person would actually say the sentence that way, whether the paragraph sounds like someone chose what to emphasize, whether any sentence exists only because formal writing is "supposed" to contain it, and whether the edit improved the whole paragraph rather than merely swapping synonyms.

If a sentence fails, rewrite the sentence as a whole instead of patching isolated words.

## Editing intensity

Light: remove filler, awkward phrasing, and repetitive transitions.

Medium: vary syntax, improve paragraph rhythm, sharpen judgments, and remove template structure.

Heavy: rebuild sentences and paragraph flow while preserving all substantive claims and evidence.

Default to light or medium unless the user asks for a full rewrite.
